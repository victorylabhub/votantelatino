If you're voting by mail, timing matters more than ever.

## Two kinds of deadlines

- In **35 states**, your ballot must **arrive** at the election office by Election Day.
- In **15 states and the District of Columbia**, ballots that arrive later still count **if they're postmarked** by Election Day. The number of extra days varies.

Check which rule applies on your state election office's website.

## The postmark change

Since **December 24, 2025**, the Postal Service has warned that the postmark date **is not necessarily the day you dropped off the envelope**. With fewer pickups and mail processed at regional centers, a ballot mailed on Election Day may be postmarked a day or more later. In postmark states, that could get it rejected.

## What to do

- **Mail it at least a week early**, as the Postal Service recommends.
- If you're mailing late, ask at the post office counter for a free **hand postmark**, dated that day.
- Close to the election, it's safer to use an **official ballot drop box** or return it to a vote center.
- If your state offers it, **track your ballot** online to confirm it arrived.

## Who gets a mail ballot automatically?

In **California, Colorado, Hawaii, Nevada, Oregon, Utah, Vermont, Washington** and the **District of Columbia**, every registered voter is mailed a ballot. In **13 states** you need a valid excuse to vote absentee; everywhere else you can request one without giving a reason.
