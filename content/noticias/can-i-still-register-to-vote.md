If you're not registered yet, don't give up: it depends on your state.

## States where you can register on November 3

You can register and vote on Election Day in **California, Colorado, Connecticut, Hawaii, Idaho, Illinois, Iowa, Maine, Maryland, Michigan, Minnesota, Nevada, New Hampshire, New Mexico, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, Wisconsin, Wyoming and the District of Columbia**.

You'll usually need to do it in person and show proof of residence. Requirements and locations vary by state, so check your election office's website before you go.

## Early voting only

In **Massachusetts, New York and North Carolina** you can register and vote on the same day, but only during early voting, not on Election Day.

## States where the deadline has passed

Everywhere else, registration closed earlier. For example, the deadline in **Texas, Arizona and Florida** was **October 5**.

## How to confirm your registration

Even if you registered years ago, it's worth checking: records are updated when you move or after periods without voting. Choose your state on [vote.gov](https://vote.gov) and follow the link to your election office.

If your name isn't on the list at your polling place, you have the right to ask for a **provisional ballot**.
