**Section 203 of the Voting Rights Act** requires certain jurisdictions to provide all election information they offer in English in other languages too.

## Where it applies for Spanish

Under the Census Bureau's most recent determinations, from December 2021, Spanish-language assistance is required:

- **statewide** in **California, Florida and Texas**, and
- in more than **200 counties and towns in 26 states**.

A jurisdiction is covered when more than 10,000 people, or more than 5% of its voting-age citizens, belong to a single language group and have limited English proficiency.

## What they must provide

If your county is covered, it must give you in Spanish everything it offers in English:

- registration materials,
- polling place notices,
- sample ballots and instructions,
- **ballots**, in person and by mail,
- and **oral assistance**, such as bilingual poll workers.

## If your county isn't covered

Even if your county isn't on the list, you can bring a person you trust to help you vote, as long as it isn't your employer or a union representative. Many election offices also provide Spanish materials on their own.

## If you don't get help

Ask to speak with the poll worker in charge and call **888-VE-Y-VOTA (888-839-8682)**. You can also report it to the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division.
