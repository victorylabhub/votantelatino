There's no national rule: each state decides which documents it asks for at the polls.

## States that ask for ID

**36 states** request or require some form of ID to vote in person, and most of them ask for **photo ID**, such as a driver's license, state ID or passport. Some also accept non-photo documents.

## States that don't

In **California, Hawaii, Illinois, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Nevada, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, Oregon, Pennsylvania, Vermont** and the **District of Columbia**, registered voters generally don't have to show ID: their identity is verified another way, such as by signature.

## First-time voters who registered by mail

Under a federal law, the **Help America Vote Act (HAVA)**, some first-time voters who registered by mail must show ID in any state if the number they gave when registering couldn't be verified.

## If you don't have the required ID

In most states that require ID, voters without it can cast a **provisional ballot** or sign an affidavit. In some cases you'll need to show ID within a few days for your vote to count.

## Before you go

Check the exact list of accepted documents on your election office's website. You can get there by choosing your state on [vote.gov](https://vote.gov).
