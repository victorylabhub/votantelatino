Tuesday, November 3 is a midterm election: there's no presidential race, but many offices that shape laws, budgets and services are up.

## Congress

- **House of Representatives:** all **435 seats** are up. Each represents a district and serves a two-year term.
- **Senate:** **35 seats** are on the ballot: 33 regular seats plus 2 special elections, in **Florida** and **Ohio**, to finish terms that became vacant.

## State governments and legislatures

- There are **36 governor's races**. In 26 states, voters choose both a governor and a U.S. senator.
- About **6,100 state legislative seats** are up in 46 states. Louisiana, Mississippi, New Jersey and Virginia have no legislative races this year because they hold them in odd-numbered years.

## Ballot measures and local races

Voters in 40 states will decide **145 statewide measures** on November 3. Depending on where you live, your ballot may also include mayors, city councils, school boards, sheriffs, judges and local measures.

## The Latino vote

Pew Research Center estimates about **39 million Latinos are eligible to vote** in 2026, or **16%** of the national electorate.

## Before you vote

Several states adopted new congressional maps since the 2024 election, so your district may have changed. The surest way to know what's on your ballot is the **sample ballot** published by your county election office. You can find yours through [vote.gov](https://vote.gov).
